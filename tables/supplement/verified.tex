We recomputed the certified interval of every Monte Carlo pair ($n=1720$) with the verified bounds of \cref{prop:verified}, taking the chain of the best curve with $T=128$. Positive definiteness was certified at every chain point (no failures). Every verified interval contains the reference value to within the reference solver's own accuracy (0 exceptions at relative tolerance $10^{-12}$). In 104 pairs, all univariate, the verified lower bound exceeds the reference value by at most $1.7\times10^{-13}$ relative. This is expected: for $d=1$ the marginal bound is exact (\cref{prop:exact}), so lower bound and reference coincide up to the rounding of the reference. The verified bounds differ from their floating-point counterparts by at most $2.4\times10^{-8}$ (lower) and $2.3\times10^{-8}$ (upper) relative to $\rho_\mathcal{N}$. The largest discrepancies occur on the most ill-conditioned pairs (whitened condition numbers up to $2\times10^9$). There floating-point whitening loses accuracy of order $\kappa\,u$, with $u$ the unit roundoff, and this is precisely where the verified enclosure is needed. In these experiments rounding never changed a conclusion, but the verified enclosure removes the need to argue this case by case.
