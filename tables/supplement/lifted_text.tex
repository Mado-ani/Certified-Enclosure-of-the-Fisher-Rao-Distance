Over all 1720 pairs, the lifted curve of \citet{NielsenPMLR2023} has discretised length ($T=128$) exceeding $\rho_\mathcal{N}$ by a median of 0.91\% and up to 133.7\%. Its discretised length at $T=128$ is smaller than that of $c_{\mathrm{CO}}$ in only 4.2\% of pairs. The recursive algorithm with $\varepsilon=10^{-3}$ misses its claimed guarantee in 78.1\% of pairs, with median error 1.7\% and maximum 183.0\%. Both methods broke down numerically on 1 pair. The lifted matrices contain both $\Sigma$ and $\Sigma^{-1}$, which squares the condition number. \Cref{tab:lifted} gives the breakdown by dimension. The errors are not monotone in $d$, but this reflects the sampling schemes rather than dimension. Dimension $4$ comes only from scheme~A, which produces strongly separated pairs (median $\rho_\mathcal{N}\approx5.5$), whereas $d=10$ comes only from scheme~B (median $\rho_\mathcal{N}\approx1.7$). At $d=5$, which both schemes cover, the median error of the recursive algorithm is $5.9\%$ in scheme~A and $0.7\%$ in scheme~B: the error grows with separation.
